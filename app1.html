Normal Distribution — Manufacturing Quality Control

A company manufactures metal shafts with a target diameter of 10 mm. Shaft diameters vary naturally and follow a normal distribution. The acceptable range is 9.90 mm to 10.10 mm. Products outside these limits are defective.

Exponential Distribution — Cloud Request Inter-arrival Time

A cloud service receives requests at an average rate of 12 requests per minute. The exponential distribution models the time between consecutive incoming requests.

Assumption: Requests arrive according to a constant-rate Poisson arrival process.
Inter-arrival time is the time between incoming requests. It is NOT server response time or processing time.

Input Parameters

Mathematical Formula

f(t) = λe−λt
P(T ≤ t) = 1 − e−λt
P(T > t) = e−λt
Mean = 1/λ
Variance = 1/λ²
P(T > t₂ | T > t₁) = e−λ(t₂−t₁)

For seconds, convert λ from requests/minute to requests/second.

Memoryless Property

Suppose you have already waited 3 seconds for a request, but it has not arrived. The probability of waiting another 5 seconds is the same as the probability of waiting 5 seconds from the beginning. The elapsed waiting time does not change the distribution of the remaining waiting time. This is called the memoryless property.

Central Limit Theorem — Warehouse Processing Time

A warehouse processes packages. Individual package processing times may vary and need not follow a normal distribution. The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of sample mean processing times becomes approximately normal as the sample size increases.

This module uses an illustrative uniform population of processing times between 2 and 10 minutes.

Input Parameters

Mathematical Formula

E(X̄) = μ
Standard Error = σ/√n
Var(X̄) = σ²/n
Z = (X̄ − μ)/(σ/√n)

As n increases, the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution, provided the population has a finite variance.
